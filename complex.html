Faith and history

How Dertour tells the story of the island’s people.

Christianity reached Cyprus through the Apostle Paul, which makes the Church of Cyprus one of the oldest independent churches in the world. Cypriots are proud of that, and many hold that their Orthodox practice is closest to the early faith.

Almost all Greek Cypriots are Orthodox, even if they rarely practise, because it marks national belonging. The church runs through an archbishopric, five metropolises and 11 monasteries, with its seat in Nicosia, and it keeps close ties to the Church of Greece.

Archbishop Makarios III led the island to independence and became president of the new republic, which made Cyprus, along with the Vatican, one of the few states ruled by a churchman.

Most Turkish Cypriots are Sunni Muslims, though often among the most secular in the world. In the north, Islam dominates. In the south, the Orthodox Church does.

  1. AD 45The Apostle Paul brings Christianity to the island.
  2. 10th centuryThe Byzantine emperor Nikephoros retakes Cyprus and revives the church.
  3. 1571The island becomes part of the Ottoman Empire, and Islam arrives.
  4. 1974Turkey takes control of the north. Dertour records that many churches and monasteries there were destroyed.

What the seasons bring

The climate is Mediterranean, with hot, dry summers and cool, wet winters.

Winter

Snow on Mount Olympus opens the ski slopes. Flamingos fill Larnaca’s salt lake from November to March, and Limassol throws its multi-day Carnival in February.

Summer

Long hot days belong to the beaches of Ayia Napa and Protaras. Limassol’s promenade and beach bars such as Guaba stay lively after dark.

All year

The ruins of Paphos, the old hill villages, the Akamas trails and Nicosia’s two halves are open whatever the weather.

How the island earns

Three industries carry the economy.

Energy

Natural-gas finds at sea have set big plans in motion. Cyprus wants to become an energy centre of the eastern Mediterranean, with links to Europe and East Asia.

Tourism

Monuments, archaeological sites and fine-sand beaches with clear water draw families and couples alike.

Shipping

Import and export are central, and the island’s position makes it a natural base for maritime services.
